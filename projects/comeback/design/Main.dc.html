The idea in one line

The return that brings the customer back.

Comeback is doorstep returns and exchanges. Retailers pay for it. Shoppers see it as one button inside the store's own return flow. A driver collects the item, brings it to the store or warehouse, and the refund or swap lands the same day.
Two things come back
The item goes back to the shelf. The shopper comes back to the store. The name carries both, and the retailer only needs to hear the second one.
Reads as a verb
"Comeback picks it up." "Sent with Comeback." It sits naturally in a sentence, a button label, or a driver's greeting at the door.
Built to be co-branded
The shopper's screen belongs to the retailer. Comeback shows up as "Return with Comeback", never as a second app they have to trust.
Mark
A "c" drawn as a return arrow, adapted from the Hugeicons Refresh01 icon (MIT). Stroke thickened, arrow shortened, opening widened so it reads as a letter. One stroke, round ends, no fill.

Voice
Say it in one breath.
"We'll come get it." Not "Schedule a convenient doorstep collection."
Promise the pickup, not the policy.
The retailer owns the return rules. Comeback owns the trip. Never explain a policy in our voice.
Talk like the store, not the courier.
Shoppers hear "your refund," never "the package." Retailers hear "your customer," never "the consignee."
To the shopper
Don't take it back. We will.
Return or exchange from your door. No box, no label, no line.
Return with Comeback
To the retailer
Turn a refund into a second visit.
One button in your return flow. Our drivers bring the item to your store or warehouse and carry the exchange out in the same trip. You set the rules. We make the trip.
