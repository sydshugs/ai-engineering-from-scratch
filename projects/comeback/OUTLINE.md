# Comeback — company outline v0.1

**You're busy. Let us help.**

Comeback is doorstep returns and exchanges. Retailers pay for it. Shoppers see it as one button inside the store's own return flow. A driver collects the item with no box or label, the shopper's credit lands at pickup, and the item goes to the shelf, to resale, or to recycling by the retailer's own rules.

Status: draft, September 2026. Positioning for the co-founders and for the YC application. Numbers are cited; everything else is a plan.

## Who it is for

- **The shopper.** Has something to send back and no time to do it.
- **The retailer.** Pays for every return today and sees almost none of the process.
- **The driver.** Comeback's workforce, on its own network or a partner fleet. Not a customer, but every promise below runs through them.

## The problems

### For the shopper

- **Time.** A return means finding a box, printing a label, driving to a store or a carrier, and standing in a line. Each step is a reason to give up and keep the thing.
- **Money.** The refund waits until the carrier scans the package, then until the warehouse inspects it. Days to weeks after the item left the house.
- **Uncertainty.** Once it is dropped off there is no view of it. Was it received? Was it accepted? Will the refund be short?
- **Consequence.** 67% of consumers say a bad return experience would stop them shopping with that retailer again (NRF and Happy Returns, 2024).

### For the retailer

- **Scale.** US retailers took back an estimated $890 billion of merchandise in 2024, 16.9% of sales (NRF and Happy Returns, 2024).
- **Cost per return.** Return shipping, receiving, inspection, restocking. An item that turns out to be unsellable still costs the full handling before it is liquidated.
- **Blindness.** The retailer learns what came back when it lands at the distribution center. Until then it is a line in a returns portal.
- **Disputes.** No evidence at the handoff, so damaged, wrong, or missing items become arguments with the customer.
- **Loyalty.** 76% of consumers say free returns are a key factor in where they shop (NRF and Happy Returns, 2024). Returns are a retention channel being run as a cost center.

### For everyone else

- **Waste.** An estimated 9.5 billion pounds of returned goods went to US landfills in 2022, producing 24 million metric tons of CO₂ (Optoro, 2022).
- **Two-leg shipping** for items that are never resold.

## The goals

What Comeback exists to accomplish, each with the measure that proves it.

| Goal | Measure |
|---|---|
| A return takes under a minute of the shopper's time and no materials. | Shopper minutes per return. Boxes and labels printed: zero. |
| Credit lands at pickup, not at inspection, for eligible returns. | Hours from pickup to refund. |
| Every item is visible from doorstep to disposition. | Handoffs with photo proof: 100%. |
| Only sellable goods reach the shelf. | Share of returns routed correctly by the retailer's rules. |
| Retailers spend less per return and keep more of the customers who return. | Cost per return against the retailer's baseline. Repeat purchase rate after a return. |
| An exchange happens in one trip. | Share of exchanges completed at the door. |

## The solutions

Each solution answers one problem above.

| Problem | Solution | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Box, label, drive, line | **Doorstep pickup** | The shopper taps "Return with Comeback" inside the retailer's return flow, picks a window, leaves the item by the door. The driver bags it and presents the retailer's return code at the counter or hub. |
| Refund waits for inspection | **Credit at pickup** | The driver's pickup photo triggers the retailer's refund for eligible items: under a value threshold, shopper in good standing. Inspection can claw back fraud. The retailer issues the money; Comeback never fronts it. |
| Blind transit | **Doorstep-to-shelf tracking** | Live driver location for the shopper. Photo at the door and photo at the counter or hub. A retailer dashboard shows every item in motion and its disposition. |
| Unsellable goods restocked | **Rules-based disposition** | The retailer sets rules per product: back to shelf, route to resale, recycle. Comeback executes the rule and reports the outcome. Comeback never makes the judgment in the moment. |
| Wrong size becomes a refund | **Exchange in one trip** | The driver brings the replacement out and takes the original back. The sale is kept. |
| Counter friction | **Store handoff protocol** | A pre-authorized return code, one-scan acceptance, and a one-page card for associates so a driver at the counter is expected, not a surprise. |
| Landfill | **Recovery channel** | Items routed away from the shelf go to resale partners or recycling. The retailer gets a recovery report; Comeback earns a share. |

## How it works

### Shopper

1. Start a return in the retailer's app or site as usual.
2. Choose "Return with Comeback" and a pickup window.
3. Leave the item by the door. No box, no label.
4. Watch the driver arrive. See the pickup photo.
5. See the credit land, for eligible items at pickup.
6. Done. For an exchange, the replacement is handed over at the same visit.

### Driver

1. Accept the pickup and the retailer's handling instructions.
2. Photograph the item at the door and bag it.
3. Deliver to the store counter or the Comeback hub, as the retailer's rule says.
4. Photograph the handoff. The item is now the retailer's, or Comeback's for recovery.

### Retailer and store

1. Turn on Comeback as a return option in the existing returns flow.
2. Set eligibility for credit at pickup and disposition rules per product.
3. Stores get the associate card and the code scanner. Drivers present the return code like any customer.
4. Watch returns in motion on the dashboard. Get the recovery report monthly.

## Business model

Three lines of revenue, in the order they arrive.

1. **Per-pickup fee.** The retailer pays for the trip. Day-one revenue.
2. **Disposition fee and recovery share.** For every item routed to resale or recycling, a handling fee plus a share of what is recovered. This is the circular economy line, and it turns recycling from a cost into revenue.
3. **Platform fee.** Dashboard, integrations, and reporting once volume justifies it.

Who owns what:

- **The retailer owns the refund.** It is their policy and their money, exactly as today. Comeback's pickup photo lets them pay it sooner.
- **Comeback owns the trip and the recovery.** If an item is lost or damaged in Comeback's hands, Comeback pays. That custody guarantee needs insurance and should be priced from day one.
- **Comeback is not an insurer.** A damaged or wrong item returned by a shopper is decided by the retailer's policy, backed by the photo.

Why the retailer says yes: they already pay for every unsellable return, then liquidate or destroy it. Comeback does not create that loss. It makes the loss cheaper to carry and recovers value from part of it.

## What Comeback is not

- **Not a carrier drop-off service.** Uber and DoorDash take prepaid packages to UPS. Comeback takes the item to the store and closes the return.
- **Not a returns portal.** Loop, Narvar, and Happy Returns manage the return decision. Comeback plugs into them and handles what happens after the decision.
- **Not a lender.** Comeback does not front refunds at launch. If that ever makes sense, it is a financed product with a partner and loss data behind it.
- **Not a marketplace.** Recovery goes through partners, not a Comeback storefront.

## Roadmap

1. **Now: the next seven weeks.** One metro, one category, three to five retailers. Twenty to fifty pickups done by the founders. A log of what stores accept at the counter. A rough shopper flow that the first twenty shoppers actually used. One retailer pilot with a name on it. Apply to YC by the deadline.
2. **Year one.** Pickup and tracking live in one metro. Driver supply through a partner fleet. First integration with a returns portal. Store handoff protocol proven with the pilot retailer. Credit at pickup with one retailer that will pay on the photo.
3. **Year two.** Credit at pickup as the default. Exchanges in one trip. Disposition rules and the first recovery partner. Second and third metros.
4. **Year three.** Recovery channel as a revenue line. Platform fee. Multi-metro network with a mix of partner and Comeback drivers.

## How we will know it is working

- Counter acceptance rate for driver-presented returns.
- Shopper minutes per return.
- Hours from pickup to credit.
- Cost per return against the retailer's own baseline.
- Share of returns routed by rule, and share that reach the shelf sellable.
- Pounds diverted from landfill.
- Repeat purchase rate in the 90 days after a Comeback return.

## Open questions

- Will stores accept a return presented by a driver rather than the buyer? This is the first thing the seven weeks must answer.
- Which retailers will pay a refund on a pickup photo, and at what threshold?
- Custody insurance: cost and provider.
- Name clearance for Comeback. A paid trademark search is needed before spending on it.
- State rules on handling and recycling returned goods, especially electronics and cosmetics.
- Driver supply: a partner fleet at launch, or Comeback drivers from day one.
